APPLY THE IDEA

Worked example

A payment service needs to support new providers. Strategy can isolate provider-specific behavior; adding a pattern without provider variation would add ceremony without solving a problem.

  1. State the change that is difficult today.
  2. Choose the smallest pattern that separates that change.
  3. Review whether tests and callers became clearer.
  4. Remove layers that have no distinct responsibility.

TRADE-OFFS

Benefits and limitations

Benefits

  • Provides a concise vocabulary for responsibilities and collaboration.
  • Can isolate change, improve substitutability, and make tests more focused.

Limitations

  • Named patterns can be over-applied and add indirection or abstractions.
  • A textbook structure may not fit the real domain or framework.

DECISION GUIDE

When to use it

Use: Use a pattern when a recurring change or collaboration problem is present.

Avoid: Avoid choosing a pattern by name before understanding the forces and constraints.

COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out

  • Avoid choosing a pattern by name before understanding the forces and constraints.
  • Assuming patterns: what they are and why they matter guarantees outcomes beyond its stated scope.

BEST PRACTICES

In practice

  • Explain both the problem and the trade-off in a review.
  • Prefer simple code until change pressure justifies structure.

INTERVIEW PRACTICE

Common questions

  1. What problem does a pattern solve beyond giving code a familiar shape?
  2. How do you decide if an abstraction is worth its cost?
  3. Can a pattern guarantee maintainability?

SENIOR-LEVEL SCENARIO

Production thinking

A team introduces factories, repositories, and mediators in a small CRUD feature. Review whether each boundary represents real variation or merely adds files and navigation.

SUMMARY

In one minute

A pattern earns its place by improving a real design constraint, not by matching a catalog diagram.
