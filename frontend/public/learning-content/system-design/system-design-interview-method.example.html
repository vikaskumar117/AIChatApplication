APPLY THE IDEA

Worked example

For a notification service, clarify channels, delivery guarantees, throughput, and user preferences first. Then estimate peak sends, separate request acceptance from delivery, and deep-dive deduplication and provider failure rather than prematurely choosing a queue.

  1. Ask whether notifications are best-effort or must be durably accepted.
  2. Estimate peak event rate and provider quotas.
  3. Draw API, durable queue, dispatcher, preference store, and provider adapters.
  4. Deep-dive deduplication, retries, rate limits, delivery status, and replay.

TRADE-OFFS

Benefits and limitations

Benefits

  • Creates a clear reasoning path from product need to implementation
  • Makes assumptions and risks reviewable

Limitations

  • Early estimates are uncertain and must be labeled
  • A checklist cannot replace domain judgment or validate a design by itself

DECISION GUIDE

When to use it

Use: Use this sequence in interviews and architecture reviews; adapt depth to the most important risk and stated constraints.

Avoid: Avoid spending most of the time on technology names before defining requirements, capacity, and success criteria.

COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out

  • Jumping to microservices or a database before clarifying the workload.
  • Treating availability, consistency, and latency as unlimited goals.
  • Ignoring failure, operations, security, and cost until the end.

BEST PRACTICES

In practice

  • State assumptions aloud and label estimates.
  • Use diagrams to show ownership and request/event direction.
  • Prioritize the hardest constraint and revisit the design when assumptions change.

INTERVIEW PRACTICE

Common questions

  1. What is the difference between functional and non-functional requirements?
  2. How do you choose which component to deep-dive?
  3. What assumptions would change your architecture?
  4. How do you explain a trade-off to a product stakeholder?

SENIOR-LEVEL SCENARIO

Production thinking

A team proposes microservices for a small new product without traffic or team constraints. Reframe the decision around independent scaling, ownership, deployment needs, and operational readiness; start with the simplest boundary that preserves a credible evolution path.

SUMMARY

In one minute

Clarify, quantify, sketch, deep-dive, and validate; the quality of the reasoning matters more than a fashionable technology list.
