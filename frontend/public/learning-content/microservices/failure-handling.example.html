APPLY THE IDEA

Worked example

An inventory conflict is returned to the user; a temporary network error on a read may be retried once; a failed event is parked for diagnosis.

  1. Define error taxonomy at each contract.
  2. Set one overall time budget across nested work.
  3. Give every failed durable item an owner and replay path.
  4. Record correlation IDs without logging secrets.

TRADE-OFFS

Benefits and limitations

Benefits

  • Correct classification avoids useless work and makes recovery understandable.
  • Durable failure paths support investigation and safe replay.

Limitations

  • More failure states require deliberate contracts and operational ownership.
  • Dead-letter queues can become silent data graveyards without monitoring and replay controls.

DECISION GUIDE

When to use it

Use: Model expected business rejection separately from transient infrastructure failure.

Avoid: Avoid catching all exceptions and retrying or returning success-shaped defaults.

COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out

  • Avoid catching all exceptions and retrying or returning success-shaped defaults.
  • Assuming failure handling across services guarantees outcomes beyond its stated scope.

BEST PRACTICES

In practice

  • Make recovery bounded and observable.
  • Make manual replay idempotent and access-controlled.

INTERVIEW PRACTICE

Common questions

  1. Which failures are safe to retry?
  2. How should a permanent rejection differ from a transient failure?
  3. What operational process is needed for dead-lettered messages?

SENIOR-LEVEL SCENARIO

Production thinking

A malformed event repeatedly fails a consumer. Stop automatic redelivery, dead-letter with context, alert an owner, fix or transform the event, then replay under controlled deduplication.

SUMMARY

In one minute

Reliable failure handling combines classification, containment, truthful outcomes, and a recovery process.
