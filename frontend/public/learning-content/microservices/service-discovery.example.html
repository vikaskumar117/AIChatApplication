APPLY THE IDEA

Worked example

A client calls `inventory` by logical name; Kubernetes DNS and a Service route to healthy replicas without embedding pod addresses.

  1. Use stable service identity and platform-native discovery.
  2. Test endpoint turnover and connection draining.
  3. Monitor resolution failures and unhealthy-instance routing.

CODE OR QUERY

Try it

var client = httpClientFactory.CreateClient("inventory");
var response = await client.GetAsync("/v1/items/42", deadlineToken);

TRADE-OFFS

Benefits and limitations

Benefits

  • Decouples callers from ephemeral instance addresses.
  • Enables scale-out and instance replacement without client redeployment.

Limitations

  • Stale DNS, health checks, and client connection pools can delay convergence.
  • A registry adds operational state and can itself become a dependency.

DECISION GUIDE

When to use it

Use: Use the discovery mechanism already supported by the deployment platform where possible.

Avoid: Avoid hand-maintained endpoint lists or assuming discovery proves business health.

COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out

  • Avoid hand-maintained endpoint lists or assuming discovery proves business health.
  • Assuming service discovery guarantees outcomes beyond its stated scope.

BEST PRACTICES

In practice

  • Coordinate discovery with rollout and graceful shutdown.
  • Avoid custom registries unless platform needs justify their complexity.

INTERVIEW PRACTICE

Common questions

  1. What is the difference between service naming and discovery?
  2. Why can DNS changes take time to affect a client?
  3. What should health-aware routing check?

SENIOR-LEVEL SCENARIO

Production thinking

After rolling deployment, clients retain connections to removed instances. Tune connection lifetime and drain behavior, and observe discovery and readiness convergence.

In one minute
