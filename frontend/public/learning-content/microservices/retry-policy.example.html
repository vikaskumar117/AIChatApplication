APPLY THE IDEA

Worked example

A safe inventory read retries one connection reset with jitter; a payment submission reuses the same idempotency key rather than issuing a new charge.

  1. Budget attempts within the caller deadline.
  2. Use jitter and respect Retry-After when applicable.
  3. Keep the same idempotency key across attempts.
  4. Measure retry volume separately from original requests.

TRADE-OFFS

Benefits and limitations

Benefits

  • Can mask brief transient network or service faults.
  • Backoff can reduce synchronized load when many callers fail together.

Limitations

  • Amplifies overload and consumes the request time budget.
  • An unknown first-attempt outcome can produce duplicate side effects.

DECISION GUIDE

When to use it

Use: Use bounded retries for transient errors on idempotent or deduplicated operations.

Avoid: Avoid retries for validation failures, unbounded loops, or calls already near their deadline.

COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out

  • Avoid retries for validation failures, unbounded loops, or calls already near their deadline.
  • Assuming retry policy guarantees outcomes beyond its stated scope.

BEST PRACTICES

In practice

  • Choose one deliberate retry owner per operation path.
  • Disable or reduce retries during overload when they worsen saturation.

INTERVIEW PRACTICE

SENIOR-LEVEL SCENARIO

Production thinking

A payment request times out after the provider may have accepted it. Reconcile or retry with the provider’s idempotency key; never generate a fresh operation identity.

SUMMARY

In one minute

Retries trade extra work and latency for transient-fault tolerance; safe operation identity and strict budgets are prerequisites.
