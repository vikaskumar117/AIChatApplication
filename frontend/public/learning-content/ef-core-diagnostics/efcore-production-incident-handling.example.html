APPLY THE IDEA

Worked example

A new release causes database timeouts; stop rollout or roll back, protect the database from retry amplification, confirm recovery, and investigate using traces and Query Store after stabilization.

  1. Declare and define customer impact, severity, and incident roles.
  2. Stop rollout or select the safest reversible mitigation.
  3. Protect dependencies from retry storms and monitor recovery indicators.
  4. Communicate status, preserve evidence, and schedule blameless follow-up actions.

TRADE-OFFS

Benefits and limitations

Benefits

  • Clear roles and reversible mitigations reduce time to restore service.
  • Evidence preservation enables durable fixes after recovery.

Limitations

  • Incident coordination consumes engineering capacity and requires practiced runbooks.
  • A mitigation can trade feature availability for system stability.

DECISION GUIDE

When to use it

Use: Use a formal incident process for material customer impact, data integrity risk, or SLO breaches.

Avoid: Avoid speculative changes, blame-focused reviews, or continuing retries that amplify a failing dependency.

COMMON MISTAKES

Watch out

  • Making multiple untracked changes during an outage.
  • Retrying aggressively against an unhealthy database.
  • Closing the incident when alerts clear without checking data integrity and backlog.

BEST PRACTICES

In practice

  • Practice runbooks and rollback procedures.
  • Maintain explicit severity and escalation criteria.
  • Communicate impact and next update cadence.
  • Review systems and process, not individual blame.

INTERVIEW PRACTICE

Common questions

  1. Who owns decisions during an incident?
  2. Why prefer reversible mitigation?
  3. What should a post-incident review produce?

SENIOR-LEVEL SCENARIO

Production thinking

A database failover increases API timeouts and retry traffic. Pause deployment, set bounded retries/circuit breaking, communicate scope, verify connection recovery and queue drain, then review failover handling and load tests.

SUMMARY

In one minute

Incident handling is a structured process to reduce customer impact, preserve evidence, communicate status, restore service, and prevent recurrence. Uncoordinated debugging during an outage can increase blast radius, lose evidence, and delay a safe mitigation. A rollback or feature disable may be better than an immediate code fix.
